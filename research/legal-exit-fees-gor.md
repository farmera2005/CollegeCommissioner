## 2. Precedents

### 2.1 Florida State v. ACC (and ACC v. FSU), Dec 2023 to Mar 2025
- **Filed.** The ACC sued first, preemptively, in Mecklenburg County, NC on Dec 21, 2023. FSU sued in Leon County, FL on Dec 22, 2023 (mem for the exact dates).
- **Claims.** FSU challenged the exit fee and the GOR, estimating the total cost of leaving at more than $572M (mem).
- **Rulings.** Mixed early rulings in both states on dismissal and stays in 2024; both cases advanced (mem).
- **Costs.**
  - FSU spent **$4.591M** in legal fees from Dec 2023 to Feb 2025 ([Rivals/Osceola](https://floridastate.rivals.com/news/fsu-s-legal-fight-with-the-acc-hit-4-95-million)).
  - The ACC reported **$12.3M+** in legal expenses for 2023-24.
  - Each side paid its own fees under the settlement.
- **Outcome.** Global settlement announced **2025-03-04**:
  - **Exit fee:** the declining schedule in §1.
  - **Media rights:** a school that pays the fee exits with its rights.
  - **Revenue split:** 60% of TV revenue distributed by 5-year rolling viewership ("brand initiative") and 40% equally among the 14 longstanding members.
  - **Cases:** all suits dismissed.
  - Sources: [CBS](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/acc-settles-florida-state-clemson-lawsuits-revised-revenue-distribution-lowered-exit-fee-among-key-details), [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/college-football/story/_/id/44108761), [FSU](https://news.fsu.edu/acclawsuit/).
- **Duration:** ~14.5 months.

### 2.2 Clemson v. ACC, Mar 2024 to Mar 2025
- **Filed.** Clemson sued in Pickens County, SC in March 2024, and the ACC countersued in NC (mem).
- **Costs.** Clemson authorized up to $975K for four law firms through June 30, 2025 ([Yahoo](https://ca.sports.yahoo.com/news/clemson-prepared-spend-nearly-1-120000286.html)).
- **Outcome.** Resolved in the same Mar 2025 settlement.
- **Duration:** ~12 months.

### 2.3 Texas and Oklahoma: early Big 12 exit (announced July 2021, agreement Feb 9, 2023)
- **Original exposure.** Leaving before the GOR ended (June 30, 2025) put them at risk of forfeiting roughly $160M combined.
- **Negotiated terms.** They left a year early, on July 1, 2024, for about **$100M of forgone distributions**, which the Big 12 itself called "an estimate based on financial distribution projections". The money went mostly to the eight legacy schools.
- **Actual cost.** Later reporting says the actual withholding was smaller than headlined. Big 12 ADs were "kind of dumbfounded" at the reduced settlement ([CBS](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/reduced-settlements-for-texas-oklahoma-joining-sec-early-leave-big-12-ads-kind-of-dumbfounded), [ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/college-sports/story/_/id/35625879/oklahoma-texas-exit-big-12-conference-2023-24-season)).
- **Separate payment.** Both schools also paid Fox to release their games a year early (amount undisclosed).
- **Duration:** ~19 months from announcement to deal. There was no lawsuit, only threatened litigation.

### 2.5 Oregon State and Washington State v. Pac-12 (control of the conference)
- **Filed** Sept 2023 in Whitman County Superior Court (WA).
- **Injunction.** Judge Gary Libey granted a preliminary injunction (Nov 14, 2023) recognizing only OSU and WSU as the Board.
- **Appeal.** The Washington Supreme Court commissioner stayed it on Nov 28, 2023. The full court **denied review on Dec 15, 2023**, leaving OSU and WSU in control of conference assets (~$400M of 2023-24 revenue at issue) ([CBS](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/washington-state-oregon-state-remain-in-control-of-pac-12-as-washington-supreme-court-denies-review-request), [Deseret](https://www.deseret.com/sports/2023/12/15/24003434/washington-supreme-court-not-hear-pac-12-lawsuit-oregon-state-washington-states/)).
- **Settlement.** OSU and WSU settled with the 10 departing schools in March 2024 (announced ~Mar 25, finalized spring 2024). The departers forgo **~$65M** of withheld distributions and share in potential liabilities. OSU and WSU keep the conference's future revenue streams, reported as ~$190M ([Spokesman](https://spokesman.com/stories/2024/mar/25/wsu-and-osu-agree-on-65-million-payout-with-10-for), [CBS](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/oregon-state-washington-state-reach-settlement-with-pac-12s-departing-members-ending-months-long-battle), [Law360](https://law360.com/amp/articles/1817390)).
- **Lesson.** The remaining members won control. Bylaws that strip departing members of board votes are enforceable.
- **Duration:** ~3 months to effective control; ~6-7 months to settlement.

### 2.6 Pac-12 v. Mountain West ("poaching penalty") and departing schools v. MWC
- **The clause.** The 2024-25 football scheduling agreement (OSU/WSU vs MWC opponents) set a penalty of $10M for the first MWC school the Pac-12 took, +$0.5M for each additional school: $43M for four schools, **$55M** with Utah State.
- **Pac-12 suit.** The Pac-12 sued in federal court in Sept 2024, arguing the clause was an unenforceable penalty and an antitrust violation ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/college-football/story/_/id/41421886/pac-12-files-poaching-penalty-lawsuit-federal-court)).
- **School suits.** Colorado State and Utah State sued the MWC over exit fees of $19-38M; Boise State joined later. The CSU board represented San Diego State and Fresno State.
- **Path to settlement.** Mediation failed in 2025, and the case went back to court. An agreement in principle was reached **May 18-19, 2026** and **finalized Aug 4, 2026** ([OPB/AP](https://opb.org/article/2026/08/05/pac-12-mountain-west-conferences-finalize-settlement-over-poaching-and-exit-fees), [ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/college-sports/story/_/id/49535459/pac-12-mwc-finalize-settlement-poaching-exit-fees)).
- **Terms** (from the settlement agreement obtained by [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/college-sports/2026/mountain-west-five-schools-settlement-payment-amount-1234942167/)):
  - **Departing schools:** $49M total, **$9.8M each**, deducted from their final 2024-25 and 2025-26 distributions. The MWC had already withheld $46.9M. This is about half of what the MWC originally sought.
  - **Pac-12 side:** OSU and WSU pay **~$10M each**. Other reports put the Pac-12 side at $22-24M against the $55M claimed.
  - **Combined:** reported at ~$69-70M ([Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/sports/unlv/unlv-football/mountain-west-to-receive-69m-from-pac-12-settlement-reports-say-3865892/), [NSN](https://nevadasportsnet.com/news/reporters/what-mountain-wests-reported-70m-settlement-figure-means-for-nevada-others-in-league)).
  - **Remaining members:** UNM, for example, receives ~$5.6M ([KUNM](https://www.kunm.org/news/2026-08-19/unm-could-receive-5-6m-from-mountain-west-settlement)).
- **Duration:** ~23 months.
- **Recovery:** ~50% of the exit-fee claim and ~36-44% of the poaching claim.

### 2.7 ACC v. Maryland (Big Ten move), Nov 2012 to Aug 2014
- **Fee raised.** In Sept 2012 the ACC raised its exit fee to 3x the operating budget, **~$52.3M**; Maryland voted no.
- **Suits.** Maryland announced its Big Ten move on Nov 19, 2012, and the ACC sued in NC that month. Maryland countersued in Maryland state court in Jan 2013, alleging antitrust violations and an unenforceable penalty.
- **Outcome.** The ACC withheld **$31.4M** of distributions. The case settled in **Aug 2014**, with the ACC keeping the $31.4M and no further payment from Maryland.
- **Duration:** ~21 months. Recovery was ~60% of the fee (mem).

## 3. Patterns

1. **Almost everything settles.** Of about 10 contested exits since 2011, none went to a final merits judgment. The only court win was a governance ruling (OSU/WSU), and it came at the preliminary-injunction stage.
2. **Recovery is about 40-60% of the headline claim**: Maryland ~60%, MWC exit fees ~50%, MWC poaching ~40%, Texas/OU ~60% of the $160M exposure (and possibly less). FSU and Clemson cut their effective exit price from more than $500M to $165M declining, roughly 25-30%.
3. **Leaving early costs a premium over the base fee.** The American charged $10M by bylaw but collected $17-18M; La Tech paid more than $8M.
4. **Leagues raise fees in response to threats** (ACC 2012, Sun Belt 2026), and schools race to leave before the vote.
5. **Home-court forum fights.** Leagues and schools both file in their home states, sometimes on the same day (ACC/FSU, ACC/Clemson, ACC/Maryland).
6. **Legal spend.**
   - P4 school: ~$3-5M per year of active litigation (FSU $4.6M over 14 months). P4 league: ~$10-15M per year (ACC $12.3M in FY2024).
   - G6 league or school: ~$0.3-2M per year.
